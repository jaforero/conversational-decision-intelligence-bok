# From the five levels of analytics to PULSE

**The five levels help you choose the analysis that serves a decision. PULSE organizes how to turn that contribution into responsible action and learning.**

The five-level article, published in January 2026, organizes the questions analytics can answer, from describing what happened to exploring data and context in natural language. The June 2026 introduction to PULSE changes the unit of analysis. Its focus moves from the analytics capability to the **decision system** that uses it. This is how the author's framework evolves. The capabilities remain available and teams select them according to what the decision needs.

## What each capability contributes to the cycle

| Analytics capability | Question it helps answer | Possible contribution to the Decision Circle |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | What happened and where did it change? | **Perceive** signals using comparable definitions, periods and denominators. |
| **Diagnostic** | What exactly changed and which explanations warrant testing? | **Focus** on the problem and distinguish findings from hypotheses about causes. |
| **Predictive** | What could happen, and how uncertain is it? | **Anticipate** scenarios that would change preparation or the timing of action. |
| **Prescriptive** | Which options work best within these constraints? | **Generate options** and support human choice with explicit assumptions and trade-offs. |
| **Cognitive** | What can we clarify by conversing with data and its context? | Explore evidence, compare options and review results at different points in the cycle, using verifiable sources and permissions. |

The table shows possible uses. The same capability can serve at several points in the cycle, and a decision may require only a subset. PULSE's five verbs, **Understand, Decide, Act, Learn and Anticipate**, express the value sought. Different capabilities can strengthen them.

## What changes when you apply PULSE

1. **Start with the decision.** PDAMR specifies the priority, the choice and its owner, the action, the metric and the risk. This determines what evidence and capability are needed.
2. **Choose a suitable capability and interface.** A report with clear definitions may suffice to make a timely decision. A forecast, optimization or conversation is justified when it resolves a material uncertainty or constraint.
3. **Close the loop.** The decision owner chooses and records what they expect the action to achieve. They then compare the expectation with the outcome and document which criterion or practice to change in the next cycle.

## Practice example · SLA compliance

**Decision.** The operations owner chooses whether to reallocate capacity to a route or keep the current allocation and review the next reporting period.

A descriptive dashboard shows the shortfall against the agreed threshold. Diagnostic analysis checks whether the signal is concentrated on one route and whether the denominator or delivery mix changed. If the evidence is sufficient for a reversible reallocation, record the decision, expectation, review date and reversal condition, such as an unacceptable deterioration on another route. If future volume could change the choice, test scenarios. A conversational interface can help users ask questions about the evidence. Learning comes from comparing the outcome with the expectation and deciding whether to revise the rule.

**Try it.** Use the SLA route in the [dashboard lab](../practice-lab/dashboards.en.md) and record the decision with the [Decision Brief template](../learn/decision-brief.en.md). The example is illustrative and the lab uses simulated data.
